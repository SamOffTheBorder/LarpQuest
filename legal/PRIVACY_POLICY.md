**API keys** — if you supply your own AI provider key, we store it encrypted at

### 3.2 Information generated by the Service

- **Chapters and narrative artifacts** generated by AI from your inputs
- **Summaries and embeddings** — numerical representations of chapter summaries
  used for retrieval. Embeddings are derived from your content and should be
  treated as personal data where the underlying content is
- **Extracted state and entity history** — an append-only log of every state
  change, retained for the life of the story
- **Validation reports, gatekeeper rulings, moderation verdicts**
- **Usage logs** — per model call: which story, which user, which model role,
  token counts, cost, success/failure

### 3.3 Information collected automatically

- **Authentication and session data** — session cookies, sign-in timestamps
- **Server and error logs** — IP address, user agent, request paths,
  timestamps, error traces. Used for security and debugging
- **Presence data** — which story members are currently online, shown to other
  members of that story. Ephemeral, not stored long-term

> **DECISION — analytics.** This draft assumes **no third-party analytics**. If
> you add any (Vercel Analytics, Plausible, PostHog, Google Analytics), you
> must list it here, describe what it collects, and — for anything using
> non-essential cookies or similar tracking — implement a consent banner that
> blocks it until consent is given. A privacy-preserving, cookieless option
> like Plausible is the lowest-friction choice.

### 3.4 What we do not collect

We do not collect precise geolocation, biometric data, government ID, or
special-category data. Do not submit such information.

> **DECISION — voice features.** `FUTURE_IDEAS.md` contemplates voice cloning
> from user-recorded samples. **Voice prints are biometric data** under BIPA
> (Illinois), Texas CUBI, GDPR Article 9, and several US state privacy laws,
> carrying written-consent requirements and, under BIPA, statutory damages with
> a private right of action. If that feature ships, this policy needs a
> dedicated biometric section and a separate written consent flow — do not
> treat it as a normal feature launch.

## 6. AI Processing — Read This

When you take an action that triggers generation, we assemble a prompt and send
it to third-party AI providers. That prompt can include:

- The universe's canon bible and rules
- The entity schema and the current state of all active entities
- Recent chapters in full
- Retrieved summaries of older chapters
- The current scene setup and **all player submissions for the turn**
- Gatekeeper rulings and canon exceptions

**Consequences you should understand:**

1. **Your writing leaves our systems.** It is processed on third-party
   infrastructure under their policies.
2. **Other players' submissions travel with yours.** A shared story's context
   is assembled from everyone's input.
3. **We request no-training handling where providers offer it, but cannot
   guarantee third-party compliance.**
4. **Automated decisions.** Moderation may block a submission and validation
   may reject a chapter automatically. These affect content, not your legal
   rights, and a GM or story owner can review and override them. If you believe
   an automated decision was wrong, contact [SUPPORT EMAIL].

## 8. Security

- Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest
- Row-Level Security on every database table, gated through story membership,
  so users can only reach data for stories they belong to
- API keys encrypted with AES-256-GCM; master key held in environment
  configuration, never in the database
- Privileged database credentials are server-side only and never exposed to the
  browser
- Background worker endpoints require a bearer secret

No system is perfectly secure. If we become aware of a breach affecting your
personal data, we will notify you and any required regulator as the law
requires — under GDPR, within 72 hours of becoming aware where the breach is
likely to result in risk to individuals.

Report security issues to [SECURITY EMAIL]. We will not pursue legal action
against good-faith researchers who report responsibly and do not access other
users' data.
