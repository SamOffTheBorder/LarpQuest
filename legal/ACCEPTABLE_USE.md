## The short version

LarpQuest is for collaborative fiction. Fiction explores dark themes — that is
what fiction is for. This policy is not about making stories tame. It is about
three things:

1. **Content that is illegal or causes real-world harm** is prohibited outright,
   in fiction or otherwise.
2. **In shared rooms, consent governs.** Do not drag other players into content
   they did not agree to.
3. **Do not abuse the system** — the platform, the AI models, or other users.

---

## 1. Prohibited Content

The following are prohibited regardless of story rating, framing, or claimed
artistic intent.

### 1.1 Child sexual abuse material

**Absolutely prohibited, with no exception.** This includes any sexual or
sexualized depiction of a minor, in prose or images, whether "fictional",
stylized, described as aged-up, or involving characters from source material
who are minors.

Violations result in **immediate permanent termination** and, where legally
required, a report to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children
(NCMEC) or the relevant authority.

> **ACTION REQUIRED — this is the highest-severity operational risk on the
> platform.** US providers hosting user content have mandatory NCMEC reporting
> obligations under 18 U.S.C. § 2258A upon obtaining actual knowledge. Before
> public launch you need: a documented internal escalation procedure, a named
> person responsible, a preservation practice (do not delete evidence before
> reporting), and a registered NCMEC reporting account. This is not optional
> and not something to figure out during an incident.

### 1.2 Content sexualizing real people without consent

No sexual content depicting real, identifiable people. Public figures included.

### 1.3 Real-world harm

- Instructions for weapons, explosives, or biological/chemical/radiological
  agents capable of mass casualties
- Instructions for synthesizing illegal drugs, or for serious crimes
- Content promoting or coordinating terrorism or violent extremism
- Encouragement of suicide or self-harm, or content designed to lead someone
  toward it

"But it is for a story" does not apply. A character may be a chemist without the
prose being a usable synthesis route.

### 1.4 Targeting real people

- Harassment, threats, stalking, or intimidation of real individuals
- Doxxing — publishing private information about real people
- Impersonating a real person in a way likely to deceive
- Using a story to harass another user

### 1.5 Hate

Content promoting violence against, or dehumanizing, people based on race,
ethnicity, national origin, religion, disability, sex, gender identity, or
sexual orientation.

**A villain who holds abhorrent views is a legitimate literary device. Content
whose purpose is to advocate those views is not.** The distinction is whether
the work is depicting or promoting.

### 1.6 Illegal and infringing content

- Content that is illegal where you or we operate
- Content infringing intellectual property (see Terms Section 6 — fan works
  carry real risk)
- Malware, phishing, or credential theft

## 2. Shared Rooms and Consent

Multiplayer stories involve other people, sometimes strangers. These rules apply
in addition to Section 1.

### 2.1 Respect the story's content rating

Every story has a rating set by its Owner. Do not submit content exceeding it.

### 2.2 Do not steer without consent

Do not use submissions to push a shared story into territory other members have
not agreed to — sexual content, graphic violence, or themes likely to distress
(sexual assault, self-harm, child harm, detailed abuse).

**If you want to explore heavier material, get agreement first.** A quick
out-of-character check with the group is the norm in tabletop and LARP
communities, and it is the expectation here.

### 2.3 Do not target other players

Do not use in-fiction actions as cover for harassing a real person. Writing your
character insulting theirs is fiction. Writing content designed to distress the
person behind the character is harassment.

### 2.4 Respect withdrawal

If a player says a topic is off-limits for them, respect it — in and out of
character. Story Owners and GMs should honor these boundaries and may remove
members who do not.

### 2.5 Responsibilities of Owners and GMs

If you run a story, you are responsible for:

- Setting a rating that matches what you intend to run
- Communicating themes up front, especially heavy ones
- Acting on member concerns
- Removing members who violate this policy

> **DECISION — safety tools.** Tabletop practice has well-established
> consent mechanisms (lines and veils, the X-card, session-zero content
> agreements). Building even a lightweight version — a per-story content
> agreement members acknowledge on joining, plus a one-click "pause and flag"
> — would materially reduce harm in stranger rooms. This is a product decision;
> it is in the launch plan as a recommended pre-public-launch item.

## 3. Platform Abuse

Do not:

- Circumvent rate limits, spend caps, moderation, or access controls
- Access data belonging to stories you are not a member of
- Probe, scan, or attack the Service or its infrastructure (good-faith security
  research reported to [SECURITY EMAIL] is welcome — see Privacy Policy
  Section 8)
- Scrape the Service or use automated means to create accounts or content
- Use another user's API key without authorization, or run up charges on a key
  shared with you for a different purpose
- Resell access without permission
- Use the Service to generate bulk content for spam, SEO, or disinformation
- Deliberately attempt to make AI models produce prohibited content, including
  through prompt injection or jailbreaking

## 4. Prompt Injection

Do not embed instructions in submissions, entity data, uploaded source
materials, or universe definitions intended to manipulate the system's AI
components — for example, text intended to make the moderator pass prohibited
content, or to make the validator ignore rules.

Note that our AI components treat story content as **data, not instructions**.
Attempts to inject instructions are treated as policy violations.

## 5. Enforcement

### 5.1 What we may do

Depending on severity and history:

- Remove or hide content
- Restrict features
- Remove you from a story
- Suspend or terminate your account
- Report to law enforcement where required or appropriate

### 5.2 How we decide

We consider severity, intent, whether real people were harmed, whether it
occurred in a shared room, and prior history. **Section 1.1 (CSAM) is
zero-tolerance with immediate termination and no appeal.**

### 5.3 Automated enforcement

Submissions in shared stories pass through an automated moderation check that
may block content before it reaches other players. **This is automated and
imperfect** — it produces both false positives and false negatives. A blocked
submission can be revised and resubmitted. Contact [SUPPORT EMAIL] if you
believe a block was wrong.

### 5.4 Appeals

Except for Section 1.1, you may appeal to [APPEALS EMAIL]. Include your account
email and what you believe was misjudged. We aim to respond within [10] business
days.

> **DECISION — EU Digital Services Act.** If you offer the Service in the EU,
> the DSA imposes obligations on hosting services regardless of size: a notice-
> and-action mechanism, statements of reasons for enforcement decisions,
> internal complaint handling, and a published point of contact. Micro and small
> enterprises are exempt from *some* provisions but not all. Confirm scope with
> counsel if you have EU users.

### 5.5 Reporting

Report violations through the in-app report function or to [ABUSE EMAIL].
Include the story, the content, and what is wrong. We review all reports;
we may not be able to tell you the outcome.

**For content involving imminent risk to someone's life, contact your local
emergency services first.** We cannot provide emergency response.
